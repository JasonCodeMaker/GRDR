## Key Findings

1. Observation: within-video slot embeddings are already highly similar before quantization, with encoder pairwise cosine around `0.65`, and the most similar pair reaches `0.7258` on train and `0.7233` on test. Quantization does not fix this; the most similar quantized pair reaches `0.8117` on train and `0.8055` on test.
   Interpretation: grounded. Slot specialization is weak even before code assignment, and quantization can preserve or sharpen that redundancy.
   Implication: the tokenizer is not behaving like four clearly separated semantic slots.

2. Observation: about three quarters of videos have duplicate last-layer codes across slots, but only about `9%` have duplicate full 3-layer tuples.
   Interpretation: grounded. This is partial collapse, not total collapse. Later residual layers recover some diversity, but the slot system is already redundant at the layer actually used for many per-slot decisions.
   Implication: “the codebook is globally alive” is not enough to conclude “the tokenizer is slot-healthy.”

3. Observation: layer-0 slot usage is extremely uneven. On train, slots 1/2/3 only use `22/21/21` codes out of `128`; on test they only use `18/19/16`.
   Interpretation: grounded. Early-slot collapse is already present and is concentrated in specific fixed slot positions rather than only in the global histogram.
   Implication: the current architecture is not merely noisy; some slots are structurally under-utilized.

4. Observation: reconstruction cosine stays high at `0.91` train and `0.90` test despite the redundancy.
   Interpretation: grounded. High pooled-feature reconstruction does not guarantee healthy slot factorization.
   Implication: the decoder objective is compatible with redundant slots, so reconstruction quality alone should not be used as tokenizer-health evidence.

## Current Conclusion

- The current MSRVTT best checkpoint does show measurable tokenizer redundancy.
- The strongest statement supported by the data is `partial collapse / weak slot specialization`, not `complete collapse`.
- The most severe issue is not global dead-code failure on the final layer; it is slot-specific under-utilization and high cross-slot similarity, especially in early layers.
